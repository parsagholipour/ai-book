# Jev on a 100-page Balanced book — evaluation of 2026-09-19

A second live evaluation of the Jev decision model, after the [50-page evaluation](jev-balanced-50-evaluation-2026-09-19.md) earlier the same day, on the book size the composed-chapters route exists for. One book was planned, generated, compiled and read; every gate's inputs and verdicts were captured; three P1 defects were fixed with regression tests and replayed against the live models. The same brief was then planned and generated a second time on the fixed code, which measured the fixes end to end and surfaced two more P1s, both fixed. Evidence lives under `.scratch/jev-balanced-100-2026-09-19/` (first run: run logs, captured rows, the three exports, the replay scripts and their JSON) and `.scratch/jev-balanced-100b-2026-09-19/` (second run).

## Jev in the composed route

Jev touches this route in three places: the dossier provenance screen (one call), cover design selection (one call), and the two questions of the chapter-edit acceptance gate (meaning, intent) on every editor change and every manuscript-read cut. The per-page review gates never run here.

**22 attempts, 13 responses, 9 HTTP 503s** ("Service temporarily unavailable"), every one recovered by the adapter's bounded retry at a cost of 3–5 s each. Responses took 0.5–1.2 s. Total Jev call time was under a minute of the 22-minute generation.

The acceptance gate ran 14 times (12 line edits, 2 cuts):

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Run as shipped | 14 | **5** (chapters 1–4, 8; contexts 57–75 KB) | 5 confident passes, 0.76–0.94 | 1 confident pass (chapter 3, 0.74); 4 `low_probability` at 0.40–0.51 | **1 of 14** (chapter 3) |
| Skipped | 8 line edits at 84–118 KB and both cuts at 286–289 KB | `context_budget_exceeded` | — | — | — |

The reviewer's own verdicts: 9 accepts, 4 retains (chapter 6: a concrete `harbor.example` certificate path replaced with generic certificates; chapter 10: an attribution shift about RFC 8446 and a compressed causal claim about Debian/Heartbleed; chapter 11: `needs_review` on meaning; chapter 12: the added location "Leuven, Belgium"). Both cuts were accepted with every removed span reviewed.

Cover selection: Jev picked `ruled-grid` at 0.39 across 50 options, escalated, and the fallback picked the same design. Provenance screen: `supported` at 0.56, escalated, and the reviewer accepted the excerpt — the same pattern as the 50-page book's valid quotation.

### P1 — Jev was skipped on most of a long book, because the gate's context grows with the chapter's position

The acceptance payload is the reviewer's payload: original, candidate, brief, notes, research notes, material, and everything the book has accumulated. The last group grows with the chapter index — `continuityNotes` went from 2.5 KB at chapter 2 to 29 KB at chapter 12, `earlierChapterSummaries` from nothing to 10 KB — so from chapter 5 on almost every comparison passed the 80,000-byte local budget the 50-page remediation introduced, and a cut's `surroundingChapters` (180–189 KB) put both cuts far beyond it. On a 100-page book the budget turned Jev off for 8 of 14 gates, and the 6 remaining gates were the short early chapters.

**Fixed** in [chapterEditAcceptance.ts](../packages/core/src/generation/chapterEditAcceptance.ts): the contextual reviewer still reads every byte, but the decision model now reads `fitDecisionContext`'s view — the same payload until it exceeds the budget, then without `DECISION_CONTEXT_OPTIONAL_FIELDS` in a fixed order (surrounding chapters, continuity notes, earlier-chapter digests, story state, research notes, material), with the dropped field names in `omittedContext` and an instruction to prefer `needs_review` where a judgment depends on them. The exact original and candidate, the brief, the reader notes, the budget and the cut evidence are never dropped, and a cut keeps its surrounding chapters because the retained passage a cut is judged against may live there — so both cuts still route straight to the reviewer, as they should. Outcomes record `decisionContextBytes` and `omittedContext`.

Offline, all twelve of the run's line-edit comparisons now fit at 57–78 KB (seven by dropping only `continuityNotes`; chapter 9 also drops the digests, story state and research notes); both cuts remain over budget. Live, the five chapters Jev never saw in the run were put to the real Jev with the fitted view ([jev-compact-replay-live.json](../.scratch/jev-balanced-100-2026-09-19/jev-compact-replay-live.json)):

| Chapter | Meaning | Intent | Reviewer's verdict in the run |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | pass 0.92 | pass 0.76 | accept — agreement |
| 6 | pass 0.90 | 0.64 → escalated | **retain (meaning regression)** |
| 9 | pass 0.91 | 0.59 → escalated | accept |
| 10 | pass 0.91 | 0.56 → escalated | **retain (meaning regression)** |
| 12 | pass 0.76 | 0.46 → escalated | **retain (meaning regression)** |

That table is the second half of the fix. **On the three edits the reviewer had retained as meaning regressions, Jev's meaning screen passed all three at 0.76–0.91.** They reached the reviewer only because the intent question was unconfident. A gate that can accept alone must not rest on that accident, so a confident double pass on a *fitted* view now records `partial_view` on the review outcome and runs the review anyway; only a double pass on the whole comparison returns before it, exactly as before on short books. A confident regression vetoes either way. Under this rule Jev on long books is a veto and a shadow measurement, not a substitute reviewer, and the shadow data is what an operator needs before promoting it.

Whether Jev should accept alone even on short books is a policy question this data bears on: across both evaluations the meaning screen has now missed three reviewer-confirmed regressions and confirmed none that the reviewer disputed. The 50-page report's "no demonstrated material meaning-changing false acceptance" was a statement about outcomes, not sensitivity.

Cost of the fix: Jev now runs on every line edit of a long book (about $0.0005 and 1–2 s each, plus 503 retries), and avoids a review only where it was already allowed to.

**Superseded after the third run** (see [After the third run](#after-the-third-run--the-p2-fixes)): the fitted view was retired. Across two 100-page books it never saved a review — a fitted-view pass could not accept, and both vetoes it issued were wrong — so Jev is now asked only about a line edit it can read whole; a cut and an over-budget comparison are the reviewer's alone.

### Where Jev helps and does not

- The **intent** question is not confident enough to matter: 1 of 5 in the run, 1 of 5 in the replay, 4 of 12 in the second run, none in the 50-page book. Its only live value is a possible veto; it has never vetoed.
- The **meaning** question is confident and, on the natural changes examined, wrong in the direction that matters (see above, and the 0.96 pass on a retained edit in the second run). It has never vetoed either.
- The **provenance** and **cover** screens escalated at 0.56 and 0.39 and the fallbacks agreed with what they leaned toward.
- **503s** are 41% of attempts in the first run (9 of 22), 53% in the second (31 of 59), 54% in the 50-page book (21 of 39). The retry hides them at 3–5 s each. This is the gateway's or the model's availability, not the integration's, and it is the largest single source of Jev latency — after the unbounded attempt fixed below.

## The other flows

### P1 — a wrong worked example no gate could see, now checked deterministically

Chapter 3's RSA decryption printed a four-step modular exponentiation with every intermediate residue wrong — `128^2 = 16384 ≡ 169 (mod 187)` (it is 115), then 81, 126 and 155 for 135, 86 and 103 — under a correct final `128^{23} ≡ 2`. The chained product `155·81·169·128 ≡ 2` is actually 123. Nothing in the route reads arithmetic: the editor is told to preserve quantities, the acceptance judge compares meaning, local QA is lexical, the manuscript read looks at structure, and the claim verifier (Premium/Ultra only) reads research notes. For a book whose brief asks for "small worked numerical examples", this is the defect a reader would remember. Every other numeric statement in the book (Diffie–Hellman with p = 23, the discrete-log and gcd examples, φ(187), the toy Shor run) is right.

**Fixed**: [arithmeticCheck.ts](../packages/core/src/generation/arithmeticCheck.ts) parses every math span for fully numeric statements — integer literals, `+ − · ×`, implicit products, powers, `\gcd`, `\varphi`, `\bmod`, `\pmod` — and evaluates each relation in BigInt, `=` exactly and `\equiv` under the trailing modulus. A statement with a variable, a decimal, an inequality, an ellipsis or an unknown command is symbolic and skipped. Measured before shipping against **every stored page in the local database (182 projects, 12,093 pages, 88 numeric statements): five findings, all on the one page, zero false positives.** [composedArithmeticRepair.ts](../apps/worker/src/generation/composedArithmeticRepair.ts) runs after the case-evidence repair through the same two-round loop, putting each finding to the editor as a `factualCorrection` whose evidence is the exact computation (`computations`), which the acceptance gate now accepts as evidence the judge may redo itself. What survives goes on the chapter report and becomes an `ARITHMETIC_ERRORS` issue at compile time, so the book publishes as REVIEW_REQUIRED with the statements on the card. A chapter whose arithmetic holds spends nothing.

Live replay of chapter 3 as published ([arithmetic-live-replay.json](../.scratch/jev-balanced-100-2026-09-19/arithmetic-live-replay.json)): one edit (31 s, $0.007) rewrote exactly the five lines to 115, 135, 86, 103 and `103·135·115·128`; Jev passed meaning 0.81 and intent 0.76 on the fitted view; the reviewer accepted under `partial_view`; findings after: none. 40.6 s end to end.

Two further facts came out of the same data. The diagram-friendly budget (300/380/480 words a page for SCIENCE, EDUCATION and HEALTH) was set on 2026-09-03 and never measured against printed sheets, while the general budget was recalibrated three times (480 → 520 → 570 against 490 printed). This book's PDF prints **496 words on a full sheet**, the same as any other prose — so 380 a page could print at most 88 of 100 even at full delivery, and a figure's room on the page was already priced separately as `figureWords`. And across forty first drafts in both books and every replay, **no compose call has returned more than 4,667 words** (asked 7,410; 6,224 came back at 4,508), so a chapter asking more than that is short by construction: this plan had five such chapters holding 53 of its 100 pages.

**Fixed**, three ways, each at the seam the measurement pointed to:

1. [chapterForms.ts](../packages/core/src/generation/chapterForms.ts): `ChapterFormRange.words` (the worker passes `chapterWordBudget(...).target`), and `sectionCountForPages(pages, words)` raises a chapter's floor so no section averages more than `SECTION_ASK_WORDS` (600) — a 4,940-word chapter is planned in 9–10 sections, not 4–8 — capped at `MAX_CHAPTER_SECTIONS` (12). The planner is told each chapter's `words` and a `sustainedShare` in place of the flat "one section of at least 40%": 40% of a short chapter, at most `SUSTAINED_SECTION_WORDS` (1,000) of a long one, because a 40% section of a 4,940-word chapter asked the writer for 1,976 words and got half.
2. [composedChapter.ts](../packages/core/src/generation/composedChapter.ts): the diagram-friendly branch of `chapterWordBudget` is gone; every adult category asks the measured 430/570/640.
3. [planner.ts](../packages/core/src/generation/planner.ts): the chapter-length rule's ceiling is `maxComposedChapterPages(input)` — `COMPOSE_CALL_WORDS` (4,600) over the budget's words a page, eight at 570, fourteen still for a picture book — and the rule now says "what needs more pages is two chapters".

The retry floor and the editor's extension are unchanged: they are the recovery, and the measurement says they were never the lever.

### P1 — the arithmetic checker called correct modular arithmetic wrong, and paid to repair it

The second run was also the first live exercise of the checker from the previous P1, and it fired once: chapter 12 (Shor) wrote "With g = 5, h = 4, and arithmetic modulo 23, one can calculate `5^1=5`, `5^2=2`, `5^3=10`, `5^4=4`, so x = 4" — the modulus stated once in prose and left out of every statement it governs, as mathematical writing does. Read alone, `5^2=2` is wrong integer arithmetic; read under 23, all three hold. The checker read them alone, raised three findings, and the chapter paid an edit round ($0.0085, 18 s, plus the acceptance review that followed it) to correct prose that was right. The editor happened to rewrite them as `5^2=25≡2 (mod 23)`, which is also right; but the second-round instruction — "replace every wrong number with the value given" — would have had it print `5^2=25, 5^3=125, 5^4=625`, and the discrete-logarithm example would have been destroyed by the gate meant to protect it. Under the repository's own rule for deterministic gates (one that fires on prose the reviewer approved is removed, not tuned) this had to be fixed before the checker could stay.

**Fixed** in [arithmeticCheck.ts](../packages/core/src/generation/arithmeticCheck.ts): `arithmeticScope` collects every modulus a section names — `modulo 23`, `mod 23`, `(mod 23)`, `\pmod{23}`, `\bmod 23`, `the modulus n = 187` — where a section is the run of text under one heading and a headingless chapter is one section. A failing `=` is excused when it holds under a named modulus its right-hand side is a residue of (so `2^{10}=1000` is not excused by a chapter that mentions "addition modulo 2"); a bare `\equiv` is read under the same moduli; a section that speaks of a modulus it never names has its failing equalities skipped, because the checker may miss an error and must never invent one. Re-measured on 12,193 stored pages (183 projects, 230 relation statements) and on both books' 27 chapter drafts: the five real findings of the first book remain, the Shor sentence produces none, nothing else fires. Seven new tests, on the sentence itself and on the ways a modulus is named.

### P1 — one Jev attempt stalled a gate for four minutes

At 18:42:12 the intent question for chapter 12's second edit was sent to Jev and answered at 18:46:14 with the gateway's "Request timed out" — **240 s** for a model whose 26 successful answers in this run took 0.7–6.6 s. The adapter passed the SDK only the caller's stop signal, so an attempt had no deadline of its own; the bounded retry and the fallback stage exist for exactly this failure but can only recover an attempt that ends. The gate, whose purpose is to be fast, held the chapter for four minutes and then got its verdict from the contextual reviewer in three seconds.

**Fixed** in [jevDecision.ts](../packages/core/src/adapters/jevDecision.ts): every attempt runs under `AbortSignal.any([caller, deadline])` with `JEV_ATTEMPT_TIMEOUT_MS` = 20 s, three times the slowest success seen across three live evaluations. The deadline firing is a `ProviderHttpError` 504 — recoverable by status, so the router retries and then falls back — and is read *after* the caller's signal and *before* the cancellation-identity check, so a stalled gateway can never be mistaken for a reader pressing Stop (which `evaluateWithFallback` rethrows rather than falling back on). Worst case for one question is now about 70 s (three 20-second attempts plus backoff) instead of unbounded. Two tests: a hung attempt becomes a recoverable 504, and the caller's abort keeps its identity while the deadline is armed.

Two questions exhausted their retries and escalated as `provider_failure` (chapter 11 meaning, chapter 12 intent); the reviewer answered both. The fitted view worked as designed: **Jev was consulted on all 16 line edits** (fitted contexts 65–80 KB from full comparisons of 100–124 KB), and both manuscript-read cuts (382–389 KB) went straight to the reviewer. The reviewer accepted 14 of 18 and retained 4.

The `partial_view` rule earned its place at once. That is the fourth reviewer-confirmed regression Jev's meaning screen has passed confidently across the three evaluations, against none it has caught. ### P2 findings

1. **The creative contract and the acceptance gate disagree about additions.** The editor's extension for chapter 12 was retained for adding "in Leuven, Belgium" — true, and taken from the planner's own episode `place` — as "unsupported by the supplied evidence". Under `creativeContract` the writer is told the notes are a starting point, not a boundary; the gate then holds the editor's additions to a grounded standard the compose draft never met. Small impact here (14 words), but it is why extensions are fragile. Either the gate should know the contract, or the contract should not apply to extensions.
2. **The planner's episode `place` reaches prose as fact.** 34 episodes, about 14 with a place; two are wrong and both were printed: Bleichenbacher "working in Zurich" in 1998 (Bell Labs) and Shor's algorithm "published in Los Alamos" (Bell Labs; FOCS in Santa Fe). The compose prompt shows `where:` beside `who:` and `when:` with no marker that it is a guess.
3. **The dossier is near-empty for a modern technical subject.** Primary-source search (Wikisource, Gutenberg, Internet Archive) produced two `extract-excerpts` calls and one provenance review for the whole book; material-first contributed one 138-word opening scene. The strategy's cost was small, but its promise — passages of the record under the prose — was not kept, and the Sources list still carries bare hostnames and fetched-but-unused documents (a 50-page P2, unchanged).
4. **Redundancy the cut cannot reach.** Chapter 5 re-derives chapter 3's whole RSA example with the same numbers; the Zimmermann telegram is told twice. The manuscript read saw it; the two deletion-only cuts it authorized removed 173 and 174 words elsewhere, because the redundant chapters sit at or under the word floor the cut constraints protect. Under-delivery and uncut redundancy are the same problem from two sides.
5. **Jev's intent question** is a call that has never changed an outcome (see above). Dropping it, or asking it only when meaning passed, would halve Jev's acceptance-gate traffic and its 503 exposure.
6. **503 rate.** 41–54% of Jev attempts across the three evaluations. Worth raising with the gateway; nothing in this repository can fix it beyond the retry and the attempt deadline it now has.
7. **Symmetrical hedging grew with the words.** The deterministic distribution check flagged 20 pages in the first run and 32 in the second (39 occurrences over 14 chapters), and "The distinction" opened 12 sentences. Longer chapters gave the writer's favourite move more room; the manuscript read does not name it. Stylistic, and the quality report carries it to the reader honestly.

### P1 — Jev vetoed the correct repair of a real arithmetic error, twice

The chapter 6 finding is the one case the whole arithmetic apparatus was built for, and the editor fixed it perfectly both times: `2^{14} ≡ 6`, `2^{15} ≡ 12`, `2^{16} ≡ 5 (mod 19)`, "the discrete logarithm of 5 to base 2 modulo 19 is therefore 16", and the later `2^{16} mod 19` reference. Jev then judged the changed numbers a **regression** — meaning 0.85 and intent 0.76 on the first repair, 0.84 and 0.79 on the second — and under the gate's rule that a confident regression vetoes without the reviewer, both repairs were discarded. The contextual reviewer, which accepted exactly this kind of correction in the first run's live replay and is told it may redo the arithmetic, never saw either. The known-wrong statement shipped, `ARITHMETIC_ERRORS` blocked the book, and the reader got REVIEW_REQUIRED with the exact sentence named. Across three evaluations these are the only two vetoes Jev has ever issued on this gate, and both were wrong: a factual correction changes precisely the quantities the preservation rubric protects, on evidence a decision model cannot weigh.

**Fixed** in [chapterEditAcceptance.ts](../packages/core/src/generation/chapterEditAcceptance.ts): on an edit that carries a `factualCorrection`, a decision-model regression is recorded with `escalationReason: "factual_correction"` and referred to the reviewer instead of returning `false`; a deletion-only cut carries no correction and keeps the veto, as does every ordinary line edit. Replayed live on the published chapter 6 through the real editor, Jev and reviewer: Jev said regression again (0.76 / 0.76), the reviewer accepted, one edit, 27 s, no finding left. One test with the book's own statement covers the referral, a reviewer that still retains, and an ordinary edit that still vetoes without a review.

### P1 — the export threw away the composed chapters and printed a model's guess

Nobody had compared the Contents page with the plan. The compile asks a model (`chapterize-export`) to regroup the finished pages into at most twelve reader chapters from 700-character excerpts, and it does so for every strategy — including the one whose whole premise is that the chapter is the unit of composition, planned with a title, composed as one piece to it, landing on its last page, and stored as a `Chapter` row every page points at. This run's export printed **twelve invented chapters over fifteen composed ones**: "Chapter 3: RSA and the Hidden Trapdoor" two pages into the composed chapter 3 ("The Public Door", pages 14–22), composed chapters 5–6 and 14–15 merged so a chapter's landing sat mid-chapter with the next chapter's opening after it, and none of the planned titles anywhere. The second run's export had done the same with nine. The call also ran on every content recompile ($0.007, 6 s).

**Fixed** in [readerChapters.ts](../packages/core/src/generation/readerChapters.ts) and [compileExport.ts](../apps/worker/src/handlers/compileExport.ts): `readerChaptersFromComposedChapters` derives the reader chapters from the rows — contiguous runs of `page.chapter` in page order, planned titles and summaries through the existing sanitizers — and the compile uses it whenever the strategy composes chapters, in every lane, before the cache and the model; it declines when a page has no chapter or a chapter's pages are not one run, and the model grouping stands in. A presentation recompile of this book now prints the fifteen planned chapters at their real boundaries (`.scratch/jev-balanced-100c-2026-09-19/recompiled/book.pdf`). Two tests in core, one on the handler (no chapterization call, the rows' chapters compiled).

- Chapter 14: "In Mountain View, California, on 10 August 2023, David Benjamin's account of Chrome's post-quantum deployment" — the Chromium post of that day is by Devon O'Brien.

## After the third run — the P2 fixes

Five of the third run's P2 findings were taken up after it. Each was re-measured against the run's own artifacts (the run log, the saved acceptance payloads, the plan, the shipped corpus) before anything changed, and four became code; the fifth is documented as a finding the code should not try to fix.

### P2 2 — cut acceptance reviews read the chapters the notes cite, not the book

The eight cut reviews carried 388–407 KB each, of which `surroundingChapters` — every other chapter's text — was 270–288 KB. The read's notes name the retained passage every time ("Chapter 3 already establishes the same distinction in the retained passage beginning 'By the time the RSA paper appeared…'"), and the reviewer's `retainedEvidence` for all seventeen removed spans quoted either the chapter being cut or the chapter the note named. `citedChapters` ([chapterCutContext.ts](../packages/core/src/generation/chapterCutContext.ts)) reads the notes for a chapter number after the book language's word for chapter — `markdownLabels(language).chapter` or the English one, in the digits of the language's script — and for quoted opening words found verbatim in a chapter, and the worker shows the reviewer only those chapters. Notes that cite nothing recognisable keep the whole book in view, so nothing is lost where the extractor is blind (a Persian read that quotes with «» and writes «فصل ۳» is read; one that does neither falls back).

Replayed over the run's eight saved payloads ([cut-context-measure.ts](../.scratch/jev-balanced-100c-2026-09-19/cut-context-measure.ts)): every cited set matches the notes by hand (ch 4 → [3], ch 7 → [3, 11], ch 8 → [3, 6], ch 10 → [9], ch 12 → [6, 9], ch 13 → [4, 15], ch 14 → [12, 15], ch 15 → [13, 14]); the payloads fall from 388–407 KB to 121–182 KB, **3,168 KB → 1,228 KB, 61% less**. The cuts cost $0.21 in all — nine `cut-chapter` calls at $0.055 and the eight reviews at $0.16 — and the reviews are priced almost entirely on input, so this is roughly $0.10 of the book's $0.73. The worker logs the cited set and the bytes it kept (`generation.composed_chapters.cut_context`).

### P2 5 and the Jev gate — whole comparisons only, meaning before intent, cuts to the reviewer

Two 100-page books had now run the fitted view, and the record is one-sided: seven confident double passes on a fitted view (four in the second run, three in this one), of which the reviewer — which had to run anyway under `partial_view` — retained two; two vetoes, both on a correct factual correction and both wrong; zero reviews saved. A view the model cannot be trusted to accept on is not worth a call. Cuts were a design gap the budget had been hiding: a cut is accepted span by span (`removedSpanReviews`), a verdict a single option cannot carry, yet nothing kept one from Jev but its size — every cut of the three books recorded `context_budget_exceeded`, and a short book's cut would have been accepted on a double pass with no span reviewed. The gate is now: Jev is consulted only for a line edit whose whole payload is within `MAX_DECISION_CONTEXT_BYTES`; a cut records `deletion_only_cut` and an over-budget comparison `context_budget_exceeded`, and the reviewer decides both. The intent question is asked only after a meaning pass — only two passes accept, a regression has already vetoed, and an uncertain meaning already goes to the reviewer, whom no intent verdict can spare; across four evaluations it answered 0.49–0.54 on most edits and never changed an outcome. On this book the rule would have consulted Jev on three of the sixteen line edits (chapters 1, 2 and 4, 64–73 KB whole; the rest were 84–117 KB) instead of all sixteen on a fitted view, and asked intent at most three times instead of thirteen; the two screens are unchanged. Jev's share (7% of time, 3.5% of spend) falls to about a fifth, spent only where a double pass can still skip a review — which on a 100-page book is the short early chapters, and on a 50-page book most of them. `fitDecisionContext`, `DECISION_CONTEXT_OPTIONAL_FIELDS`, `partial_view` and the `decisionContextBytes` / `omittedContext` outcome fields are gone. The factual-correction referral is unchanged.

### P2 4 — the dossier waited on a dead catalogue

The run log has no dossier events (its `research.search` rows are the model's research adapter), so the dossier's own hosts were probed live with the worker's fetcher. Wikisource answers a search in 0.5 s and archive.org in 1 s; **Gutendex answers `/books?…` with a 301 in 10–19 s and the slashed `/books/?…` it points to in 21–30 s.** The fetcher allows 15 s a request and follows the redirect, so every episode search (one an episode, three a chapter, three chapters at once — up to 45) waited on Gutendex to the request timeout, the redirect and then its target, and the phase ran to its budget: 249 s between the episode plan's answer (71 s) and the first form plan (320 s), 15% of the 1,633 s run, for four documents and two excerpts. Now ([primarySources.ts](../packages/core/src/generation/primarySources.ts)) the address carries the slash; each catalogue search is waited for `searchTimeoutMs` (10 s, twenty times the healthy hosts' latency; a late answer is dropped); a catalogue that times out twice running is skipped for five minutes, the rest of a book; and one `dossier.search` log line per query records every host's outcome and latency, which the run had nothing to show. Web discovery, a model call with its own budget, is waited for whole. Expected effect on this book: the third search onward pays nothing for Gutendex, so the phase is bounded by Wikisource's two-second cadence and the fetches — about a minute instead of four. The yield problem (P2 3 of the second run: two excerpts for a modern technical subject) is untouched; this fix only stops paying for nothing.

### P2 1 — the compose ceiling is a guarantee

Told "from five to eight pages" and "what needs more pages is two chapters", the planner still wrote three nine-page chapters into this run's plan (`[7, 6, 9, 5, 7, 6, 9, 5, 7, 6, 9, 5, 7, 6, 6]`), each asking 5,130 words of a call that returns at most ~4,600. `normalizePlanPageTargets` now takes `maxChapterPages`, and `capChapterPages` moves the excess a page at a time from the longest chapter to the longest chapter with room — the run's plan becomes `[8, 6, 8, 5, 8, 6, 8, 5, 8, 6, 8, 5, 7, 6, 6]`, the fives untouched — and splits the longest chapter in two only when the chapters cannot hold the book under the ceiling (two twenty-page chapters for forty pages become five of eight). The planner passes the ceiling for every book whose chapter is the unit of composition, by the same predicate the strategy router chooses by (`composesByChapter`, in `compositionUnit.ts` so the router stays light); a per-page book's chapters are storage and keep their plan.

### P2 6 — symmetrical hedging: the detector is right and the lever is not a prompt

Measured over the 102 shipped English books in the local database (11,086 pages, [hedge-measure.ts](../.scratch/jev-balanced-100c-2026-09-19/hedge-measure.ts)): the family fires on 1,277 pages through "does not prove / establish / mean…", 629 through "neither…nor", 302 through "may…while…may", 276 through "not…by itself", 63 through "not merely…but also". Read sentence by sentence in this book, every hit is what the detector says it is — "Bob's message containing X does not prove that it came from Bob", "the appropriate response is neither panic nor dismissal", "a smart card may reveal a secret through current draw, while a virtual machine may expose a neighbour's cache access pattern" — and the plain-negation member alone accounts for two thirds of the flagged pages (30 → 10 without it), because "correctness does not prove security" is this book's thesis, stated as often as a cryptography book states it. The warning is non-blocking ("review recommended") and the quality report carries it honestly. The lever is not a prompt: the 2026-09-03 ladder ([ladder-report-2026-09-03.md](composed-chapters/ladder-report-2026-09-03.md)) halved the couplet twice by rule (rungs 1 and 4, 27–34 → 5–8 per thousand sentences) and the blind panel did not move either time — the readers' pattern lists simply named the sibling moves the counter did not reach, the paired-antithesis closer among them — and "every rule about shape the writer or editor is shown is performed on schedule, and the blind panel names the performance" is already an invariant. Not changed; recorded here so the next reader does not re-run the same experiment.
