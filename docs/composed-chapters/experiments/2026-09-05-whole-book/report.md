## Candidate 1: one shared set of cases

The form planner previously received no episodes despite running after episode selection. It now receives those assignments, and the writer/editor see compact reservations for other chapters. The episode planner is asked to assign each full case treatment one home. No extra provider calls or changed word budgets were introduced. One initial file-size failure was corrected by moving the editor's material rules into the existing material helper. Both check logs are retained.

The original request, 120-page outline, balanced Luna low writer, flat stance positions and quality revision 49 were preserved. `bookDevelopment`, `caseEvidence` and `developmentalEdit` stayed disabled. The book completed all 15 chapters and 120 stored pages, with 52,472 exported prose words and 106 PDF pages. The generation job took 23.03 minutes. No source file changed during generation.

The live form-planner payload contained all 30 assigned episodes, and writer requests contained reservations for other chapters. The old Chapter 6/7 Mongol duplication disappeared: Chapter 6 discusses Norman conquest and Jerusalem, while Chapter 7 discusses Temujin and Novgorod. This is a verified structural repair, but it did not raise the complete book beyond the previous plateau.

They also questioned unsupported scene details. The parent's trace inspection found that the Magdeburg and Maji Maji scenes had no supplied source excerpts but produced 773 and 753 words. The Cajamarca scene's three excerpts concerned an author's working habits, quipus and court theatre, not the narrated capture. Source presence alone is not episode grounding.

Every reader recorded all 15 chapters, and all 54 highlight/pattern quotations matched the manuscript verbatim. Reader A described “many hundreds of pages” in its summary, which is not supported by the actual 106-page export and is excluded from the parent's findings; the score and original verdict remain unchanged. This reinforces that the judges assess text, not layout or measured length.

## Candidate 2: chapter questions and stricter reconstruction

Distinct chapter questions and investigation topics were added to the existing episode-planning response and passed through the form planner, writer and editor. Focused chapters received those assignments instead of the same global stance positions as repeated demands. All 383 snapshotted production files remained unchanged, live quality revision stayed 49, and the three experimental development gates stayed disabled. The plan contained 45 episodes and 15 focuses. Logged generation/export calls were all Luna: 77 calls costing **$0.430390**, including zero separate scene calls. Twenty subsequent voice-candidate calls cost **$0.023653**, for **$0.454043** in final project provider logs. Parent/Sol usage and unpriced services are excluded. Fable's bounded implementation session cost **$5.73657925**, including ancillary usage, and hit its budget cap after writing the files; its full logs and parent corrections are retained.

Three fresh Sol readers scored **6.7, 6.8 and 7.1**, an unrounded mean of **6.8666666667**. All 54 quoted highlights/pattern instances were independently checked against the manuscript, and all three coverage records contain every chapter. Pacing averaged **4.67**, slop resistance **4.33**, and voice and engagement **6.33** each. This attempt does not demonstrate improvement over candidate 1. A single-book difference of 0.23 is too small to isolate an effect from generation and reader variation, but the intervention clearly failed the 8.0 target.

The readers independently describe the same architecture: three cases, evidence qualifications, an administrative chain, then a comparative recap. The implementation's fixed three-or-four episode request produced exactly three in every chapter. More importantly, the writer was still told to give every episode a real stretch and to follow per-section form and word quotas; the editor received those quotas again. Parent inspection confirmed the resulting repetition in Chapters 9, 11 and 15. Source caution is valuable where it changes an inference; repeatedly restating it is a pacing defect. The absence of separate scene calls is a verified execution change, not proof that every historical claim is grounded or that narrative quality improved.

Explicit paragraph-shape targets were also removed from that path; legacy and narrative behavior remained. This introduced no additional model calls.
